# AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations — Notes

**Course:** Claude Academy | **Completed:** Sept 20, 2026 | **Track:** CCA-F prep (1 of 7)

## Why this framework, not just "prompting tips"
Most AI guidance focuses on prompt tricks that go stale with every model update. This framework is model-agnostic — it's a durable way of *thinking* about human-AI collaboration (decide → prompt → evaluate → own) that holds regardless of which AI system you're using. AI fluency here isn't about tool mastery; it's about being a responsible, effective human in the loop.

## The 4Ds, in depth

### Delegation — deciding what goes to AI
- **Problem Awareness**: Understand your own goals and what "success" looks like *before* opening an AI chat window. Skipping this leads to vague asks and vague outputs.
- **Platform Awareness**: Know what a given AI system can and can't reliably do — its knowledge cutoffs, hallucination risk, strengths (speed, pattern recognition) and weaknesses (judgment, context outside training).
- **Task Delegation**: With clear goals and a realistic sense of the tool, decide the actual division of labor — not "automate everything" but the most effective partnership.

*My example:* Before starting a recent writing task, I decided upfront which parts I'd let AI draft (structure, first-pass framing) versus which needed to stay mine (judgment calls, final framing). Making that call *before* prompting — rather than deciding ad hoc mid-task — is the shift this module argues for.

### Description — communicating to get useful results
- **Product Description**: Specify the desired output — format, tone, length, audience.
- **Process Description**: Guide *how* the AI should approach the task — a framework to follow, steps to take, or "think before answering."
- **Performance Description**: Define how the AI should behave during the interaction (e.g., critical reviewer vs. supportive brainstorming partner).

*My example:* Early prompts I wrote only covered Product Description ("write X"). Outputs were generic. Adding Process Description — asking it to first list considerations, then address each one — noticeably improved specificity and usefulness.

### Discernment — evaluating outputs critically
- **Product Discernment**: Is the output itself accurate and useful?
- **Process Discernment**: Was the reasoning behind it sound — any logical gaps or unjustified assumptions?
- **Performance Discernment**: Did the interaction style actually work for what I needed?

*My example:* An AI-generated answer looked complete but quietly rested on an assumption that didn't hold in my actual context. Caught it by asking the AI to explain its reasoning, not just checking the final output — Process Discernment rather than Product Discernment alone.

### Diligence — responsibility and transparency
- **Creation Diligence**: Be thoughtful about which AI systems/tools you use and how.
- **Transparency Diligence**: Be honest about AI's role in the work with anyone who needs to know.
- **Deployment Diligence**: Take accountability for verifying and vouching for outputs before they go out.

*My example:* When sharing AI-assisted work with others, I try to be upfront about which parts were AI-drafted versus fully my own — Transparency Diligence — rather than letting it appear entirely manual.

## Three modes of AI interaction
- **Automation**: AI executes a specific task based on specific instructions (e.g., "draft this section").
- **Augmentation**: AI and human collaborate as thinking partners — iterating together, not just handing off.
- **Agency**: AI is configured to independently handle future tasks on your behalf, with human oversight.

*Where does most of my current AI use sit, and where should it?* Mostly Automation right now (ask for a draft, review it). The Description-Discernment loop the course describes suggests moving toward more Augmentation — genuine back-and-forth during drafting, not just a single handoff — could improve output quality further.

## Open questions / things I want to test next

- Where Agency (AI acting independently on my behalf) might genuinely be appropriate vs. where it's premature trust.

## How this changes what I'll do differently
Before starting an AI-assisted task, state the Delegation decision explicitly (what's AI's job, what's mine) upfront — and default to disclosing which parts of any shared output were AI-assisted, rather than treating that as optional.
